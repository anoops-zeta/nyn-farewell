built on vocabulary that's actually in your data — nyn, Thursday Club, Synapse, Cipher,
CC Lego, Malare, the flat tyre, the turf — so they should sound like they came from inside
the building.

The design rule, taken from Maggie's transcript: **his land late.** A good one takes about
four seconds. Anything that lands instantly is a quip, not a PJ.

## The engine, finally

Vinay M explains what the PJs actually *were*:

> "I still remember those days when I used to be in larger staff meetings on Plutus, and just
> when the moment was getting a little too serious you used to crack a PJ… that used to burst
> out all the stress in the system."

They were tactical. He deployed them at peak tension, on purpose, to break a room. Combined
with Maggie's observation that they land four seconds late, that's the full picture: **a
deliberately terrible pun, fired at the worst possible moment, that nobody gets until the
meeting has already moved on.**

Every PJ moment on the site should be staged that way — not ambient wordplay, but a pun
dropped into a silence. The PJ Support Group now turns on this: Vinay points out the pattern,
and Arup realises they've been managed with puns for three years.

# Rewrite — simpler

The first pass failed. "Graceful degradation", "load bearing", "deprecating myself",
"out of scope", "backlog" — none of those are puns. They're engineering jargon used
metaphorically. Only engineers get them, and even they don't groan.

**The rule now: one common word, two everyday meanings, and anyone in the room gets it four
seconds later.** If you need to work at a company to understand it, it's not a PJ.

## What replaced what

| Old (jargon) | New (plain) |
|---|---|
| Call it a graceful degradation | Call it **declined** — try again later |
| We were just at a low point | Tyre's flat. Don't worry, I'm not feeling **deflated** |
| Ten felt like bragging | Because **ten was taken** |
| That's not growth, that's load bearing | Ten million **accounts**. And I still can't **count** on any of you |
| I've had practice stopping things going through | Three years of product reviews. I'm very good at **saving** |
| Deprecating myself, support ends Friday | It's not goodbye, it's **fare-well**. And this time the fare is on me |
| I'm still in scope until Friday | I'm still on the **payroll** till Friday |
| A backlog that grows on its own | Growth that's actually **organic** |

Kept, because they already followed the rule: *Wednesday was too weak*, *stay on top of
things* (Surface), *I'll need a re-verse* (singing), *Triumph — the only guaranteed win*.

## The new callback

**"Declined"** replaces "graceful degradation", and it's better in every way — it's a
payments company, so everyone gets it instantly, including the non-engineers.

1. **GFF War Room, 2024** — demo dies: *"It hasn't failed. It's just been declined — we'll retry."*
2. **PJ Support Group** — Divyansh recalls it: *"I have never been angrier about being calmed down."*
3. **His last self-chat message** — *"Don't call it an exit. Call it declined — try again later."*

Which also reads, if he wants it to, as leaving the door open.

## Best new one

Manoj now asks why he does it mid-review. The answer:

> "Someone has to break the ice. The AC is already on."

Office, universal, needs no context, and lands late. Maggie counts the four seconds; Arup
gets there last.
